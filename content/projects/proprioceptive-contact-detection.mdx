## The problem

Robots working around people or manipulating unknown objects need to detect
contact. Wrist force/torque sensors solve this but add cost, weight, and only
measure forces at a single location.

An alternative is to infer contact from joint motion: if the robot's dynamics
predict one motion but the encoders report another, the difference must come
from an external force. The challenge is that real robots never have perfect
models—payloads change, friction drifts, and inertial parameters are inaccurate.

This project asks a practical question: **can reliable contact estimation still
work with a deliberately imperfect dynamics model?**

## Key idea

Instead of chasing a perfect physics model, the estimator combines:

1. A hand-derived analytical dynamics model with **deliberately incorrect**
   masses, centers of mass, and damping.
2. A small neural network trained **only on free-space motion** to compensate
   for model error.

Since the network never observes contact, any remaining discrepancy during
interaction must be explained by an external-force state inside an Extended
Kalman Filter (EKF), producing both a torque estimate and its uncertainty.

## Architecture

The system uses two independent models:

- **Plant** — a MuJoCo simulation generating realistic sensor data and ground
  truth.
- **Filter model** — the imperfect analytical model with its learned
  free-space correction. It has no knowledge that the block exists.

![Two-model architecture: the plant (red) is hidden from the filter's own imperfect model (blue); the gap between them becomes the external-torque estimate.](/projects/contact-detection-architecture.png)

Only noisy joint position and velocity encoders are available—there are no
force or contact sensors. Performance is evaluated on continuous torque
estimation, binary contact detection, and cross-seed validation.

## Implementation

- Symbolic Euler–Lagrange dynamics (`sympy`) integrated with RK4, plus a small
  feed-forward residual network trained on free-space trajectories.
- Augmented-state EKF with Joseph-form covariance updates and an analytical
  Jacobian for external torque.
- Symbolic common-subexpression elimination (`cse=True`) reduced process-model
  execution from slower than MuJoCo to under 2 ms, enabling real-time operation.
- Fully deterministic pipeline with 182 regression tests covering estimation,
  detection, and baseline comparisons.

## Results

- **Reliable contact detection:** ROC AUC **0.99** with roughly **17×**
  detection SNR across seeds.
- **Accurate despite model mismatch:** contact-torque RMSE is within **4%** of
  the same EKF using the true dynamics (0.047 vs. 0.045 N·m).
- **Low false alarms:** **0–1.1%** false-positive rate across held-out seeds.
- **Outperforms a classical momentum observer**, even when the observer is
  given the simulator's true dynamics.

![ROC and precision-recall curves at three operating points along the false-alarm / missed-contact trade-off.](/projects/contact-detection-roc-pr.png)

## Limitations

The main weakness is **contact transitions**. Torque changes faster than the
EKF assumes, causing temporary uncertainty miscalibration. Measuring genuine
false positives also required filtering out contact chatter and EKF startup
transients.

## Future work

- Temporal detection using short evidence windows.
- Adaptive uncertainty during contact transitions.
- Estimating contact location, not just net joint torque.
- Validation on real hardware.
