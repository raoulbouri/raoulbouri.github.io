## The problem

Robots working near people need to detect contact. Wrist force sensors do this, but they add cost and weight. They also measure force at only one point.

Another option is to infer contact from joint motion. If the dynamics model predicts one motion and the encoders report another, an external force must explain the difference. The catch is that real robots never have perfect models. Payloads change, friction drifts and inertia is inaccurate.

So the question was practical: **does contact estimation still work with a deliberately imperfect model?**

## Key idea

The estimator combines two parts.

1. A hand-derived dynamics model with **deliberately wrong** masses, centers of mass and damping.
2. A small neural network trained **only on free-space motion** to correct the model's errors.

The network never sees contact. So any mismatch during contact has to be explained by an external-force state inside an Extended Kalman Filter. That gives both a torque estimate and its uncertainty.

## Architecture

The system uses two separate models.

- **Plant.** A MuJoCo simulation that produces sensor data and ground truth.
- **Filter model.** The imperfect model with its learned correction. It doesn't know the block exists.

![Two-model architecture. The plant (red) is hidden from the filter's own imperfect model (blue). The gap between them becomes the external-torque estimate.](/projects/contact-detection-architecture.png)

The filter sees only noisy joint positions and velocities. There are no force or contact sensors.

## Implementation

- Symbolic Euler-Lagrange dynamics in `sympy`, integrated with RK4, plus a small residual network.
- An augmented-state EKF with Joseph-form covariance updates and an analytical Jacobian.
- Common-subexpression elimination cut the process model to under 2 ms, fast enough for real time.
- A deterministic pipeline with 182 regression tests.

## Results

- **Reliable detection.** ROC AUC 0.99, with about 17× detection SNR across seeds.
- **Accurate despite the wrong model.** Torque error is within 4% of the same filter given the true dynamics (0.047 vs 0.045 N·m).
- **Few false alarms.** 0 to 1.1% false positives on held-out seeds.
- **Beats a classical baseline.** It outperforms a momentum observer, even one given the simulator's true dynamics.

![ROC and precision-recall curves at three operating points.](/projects/contact-detection-roc-pr.png)

## Limitations

The weak spot is the moment contact starts or stops. Torque changes faster than the filter assumes, so its uncertainty is briefly miscalibrated. Measuring true false positives also meant filtering out contact chatter and startup transients.
