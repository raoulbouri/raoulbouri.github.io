## The problem

A robot arm that only has joint encoders can't obviously tell when it has touched
something. Force/torque sensors are the usual answer, but they add cost, wiring,
and failure points. The question here: **can you detect and estimate external
contact torque from proprioception alone?**

## Motivation

The interactive arm at the top of this site is a nod to exactly this: when you

## The key engineering decision

Instead of chasing a perfectly accurate dynamics model, I used a **deliberately
imperfect analytical model** and paired it with a neural-network residual trained
**only on contact-free data**. The insight: if the correction has only ever seen
free-space motion, then any disagreement between model and reality *during*
contact must come from an external force. That disagreement becomes an explicit
external-torque signal for the filter to estimate.

## Architecture

- **Filter model** — intentionally-wrong analytical dynamics + a learned
  free-space correction (residual network).
- **Plant** — full physics simulation (MuJoCo 3.9), hidden from the filter,
  producing ground truth.
- **Augmented EKF** — estimates joint state *and* external torque; contact shows
  up as the residual the free-space model can't explain.

## Implementation notes

- Python 3.13 (ARM64), dependencies managed with `uv`.
- Fully seeded and deterministic — datasets and trained weights regenerate from
  scripts rather than being committed.
- Regression suite in `pytest` covering estimation, binary detection, and
  baseline comparison.

## Results

- Estimation error sits near the sensor-noise floor **despite the deliberately
  wrong model**.
- Uncertainty estimates are statistically consistent across most conditions.
- **Faster contact detection than classical momentum observers** — even ones
  given perfect dynamics.
